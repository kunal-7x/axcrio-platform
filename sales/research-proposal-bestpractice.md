## 0. THE ONE DISTINCTION THAT CHANGES EVERYTHING

A **pitch deck** sells equity to an *investor* (problem→market→TAM→team→ask). A **sales
proposal** sells a *product* to a *customer* and must do ONE job: move a business owner from
"interesting" to a confident **yes**. Different center of gravity:

- Deck center = **the company's upside** (market, moat, ask).
- Proposal center = **the buyer's outcome** (their pain, their ROI, their risk removed).

So this proposal is **outcome-first and buyer-centric**, not company-first. The hero is the
prospect's business, not ours. Everything below follows from that.

deliberate — it mirrors the buyer's emotional journey: *feel seen → feel the pain → see the cure →
believe it → quantify it → trust it → see the price as small → know it's safe → know the next step.*

for the champion who reads deeply.

## 2. THE CONVERSION PSYCHOLOGY PLAYBOOK (why each move works)

- **Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS).** People act to avoid pain more than to chase gain. Most sellers
  skip *agitate* — they name the problem then jump to the cure. Don't. Sit in the pain (§3) and
  quantify it before relieving it (§4). This is the single biggest copy lever.
- **Loss aversion (~2×).** Frame the status quo as an active, ongoing *loss* (leads bleeding,
  telecaller attrition, ad money wasted on non-buyers), not just a missed gain. Top reps frame the
  proposal around what the buyer **loses by not acting**.
- **Risk-reversal beats price.** Apollo 2026: the #1 reason B2B deals stall is **fear of change**,
  not price/timing/competition. The buyer intellectually wants it but is emotionally paralyzed.
  Cure = guarantees, free pilot, no lock-in, "see outcomes before you scale." Make saying yes feel
  reversible.
- **Anchoring (Kahneman/Tversky).** The first price shown sets the reference for every price after.
  Show the **human-team cost first** (₹ lakhs/mo of telecaller + marketer + attrition) so the
  product price feels small; in the pricing table show the **highest tier first** as the anchor.
- **Decoy + center-stage (3 tiers).** A deliberately weaker option lifts premium-tier selection
  35–50% (Duke 2023). Three tiers convert ~1.4× vs two; people disproportionately pick the
  middle — so make the **middle the hero** ("Most Popular" badge, visual emphasis, recommended).
- **Social proof (real only).** 79% of B2B buyers rely on social proof; testimonials rated the #1
  content type by 89% of B2B marketers; up to 34% conversion lift. BUT 6/10 buyers distrust seller
  claims — so use **verifiable, specific** proof (live URL, real call counts, real artifacts), never
  invented logos/quotes. For Famit: "it's live, here's the real call, here's the real banner" is
  stronger than any fake testimonial.
- **Effort reduction.** Every visible "complexity" raises perceived risk. Fast/clear onboarding,
  a 3-step "how it works," and a mutual action plan all lower the felt cost of changing.
- **One CTA.** Decision fatigue kills closes. End on a single, confident next step.

## 3. ROI / VALUE FRAMING — the numbers to build the calculator on (REAL ranges, India)

Use these as **honest ranges/inputs the buyer adjusts**, not as fabricated guarantees. Sources are
2026 India market data (caller.digital, qcall.ai, digibrood) + Famit's own real meter.

- **Human telecaller, fully-loaded:** ~₹40–120 per *resolved contact* (clusters ₹55–85). A
  productive caller does 75–110 dials/day, 32–45% connect, ~18–32 resolved/day → ~470–780/mo.
  Plus **80–120% annual attrition**, training, sick days, 8-hr cap, no nights/festivals.
- **AI voice agent:** ~₹12–25 per resolved contact; ~₹2–6/min (beats a Tier-3 BPO's ₹4–5/min
  fully-loaded). 24/7, instant first-touch, scales to peaks (festive/regulatory 3–8× spikes no
  staffing model can serve), multilingual.
- **Famit's own real meter:** ~₹68/mo observed per-call billing in production; AI banners ~₹10
  each — concrete, verifiable unit economics to anchor the calculator's per-unit costs.
- **The headline reframe (anchor):** a 50-seat call centre ≈ ₹20L/mo can be replaced by AI for
  ~₹4–5L/mo → ~₹15L/mo saved, 300–400% first-year ROI. Scale this DOWN to the SMB: "one telecaller
  costs you ₹X/mo and goes home at 6; Famit calls every lead in 5 seconds, all night, for less."
- **Speed-to-lead lever:** calling a fresh lead in the first 5 min vs an hour later is a ~10× swing
  in conversion — Famit's instant AI call captures the window a human always misses.
- **Honest boundary (builds trust):** humans still win on high-empathy/distress and on closing
  high-ticket (>₹50k BFSI/real-estate) deals — so position Famit as *handle the volume + qualify +
  follow up + book; hand the hot, high-ticket ones to your human, warm, with a full AI summary.*
  Saying this OUT LOUD increases credibility (we don't over-claim).

**Calculator inputs (sliders/fields):** leads/month, avg deal value, current close rate, current
cost-per-lead. **Outputs:** extra leads contacted, extra bookings, monthly saving vs a human team,
payback period (weeks), 12-month ROI. Show the math transparently — never a black-box "10× ROI!".

## 4. PRICING PRESENTATION — exact rules

1. **Anchor before price.** Immediately above the table, show the cost of the human team being
   replaced (the big ₹-lakh number). Every plan then reads as a bargain.
2. **Three tiers, highest first** (left→right) so the eye anchors high. Middle tier = the **hero**:
   "Most Popular," scaled up, accent border, recommended copy. Optional 3rd as the decoy that makes
   the hero obviously the smart pick.
3. **Outcome-named plans, not feature dumps.** e.g. *Launch / Growth / Scale* — each headline says
   the outcome ("for businesses doing X leads/mo"), with a short value list, not a 30-row matrix.
4. **One price per plan, monthly framing**, plus "≈ less than one telecaller's salary." Avoid
   overwhelming full pricing tables (Dock) — link/expand details, don't front-load them.
5. **Visible value before price on the eye-path:** ROI section sits *before* pricing so they see
   the gain, then the (smaller-feeling) price.
6. Good design + clarity alone lifts pricing-page conversion 30–50% with no price change — so this
   table must be the most polished component in the file.

## 5. TRUST / CREDIBILITY ELEMENTS (real, never fabricated)

- **"It's live" proof** > everything else for a young product: the live URL (panel.famit.in), real
  call counts (96), real campaigns (8), a real generated banner shown inline, the real per-call
  meter. Verifiable specifics beat generic claims (6/10 buyers distrust seller promises).
- **Security/compliance as trust signals** (procurement checklist items even for SMBs): multi-tenant
  Postgres + Row-Level-Security isolation, PIN + risk-tiering + immutable audit on every money/bulk
  action, DND/consent respected, the Foundation Control Layer. Frame as "your data and your spend
  are sandboxed and gated."
- **Honest limits** (the empathy/high-ticket handoff) = a *credibility multiplier*. Naming what we
  DON'T do removes the "too good to be true" reflex.
- **Founder accessibility / mutual action plan** = the human-trust layer for an SMB buyer.
- **NEVER** invent customer logos, testimonials, star-ratings, or metrics. If we lack a metric,
  show the live artifact or the transparent unit economic instead. Fabricated proof is the fastest
  way to lose an SMB owner who can smell it.

## 6. PREMIUM VISUAL + INTERACTIVE PLAYBOOK (Linear/Stripe/Ramp quality)

**Aesthetic direction (humanist-tech, restrained):**
- Generous white space, editorial type scale, single-line headings, one accent (Signal blue
  `#2A85FF`). Inter Display / Gilroy. NOT a cheap gradient template — restraint reads as premium.
- **Aurora / soft-gradient hero** used sparingly (Stripe/Linear/Vercel signal sophistication with
  it) — one subtle aurora wash behind the hero + the loop diagram, nothing more.
- Bento-ish but organic: asymmetric, breathing layout over rigid 12-col grids. Crisp 1px hairlines,
  subtle elevation, large numerals for the proof/ROI stats.
- **Dark-on-light primary, with the logo swapped to white-trim on any dark band.** Embed the real
  logo as base64 (file is self-contained/offline).

**Motion (subtle, conversion-guiding — NOT decorative):**
- Scroll-triggered fade/rise-in on each section (intersection observer), reduced-motion safe.
- Hover cues on CTAs and pricing cards (lift + accent). Animated counters on the proof/ROI numbers.
- The **animated revenue-loop diagram** as the signature moment: nodes light up in sequence
  (Ad → Lead → Call → WhatsApp → Book → Repeat) on scroll. This is the "wow" that keeps them on page.
- Performance budget mindset: inline CSS/JS, no heavy libs, fast LCP — it must open instantly and
  print clean to PDF.

**THE 5 INNOVATIONS TO INCLUDE (what makes it feel world-class + modern):**
1. **Interactive ROI calculator** — live sliders (leads/mo, deal value), instant payback + 12-mo
   ROI + "human team vs Famit" delta. The single highest-converting interactive element (Qwilr/
   PandaDoc both lead with this; lifts win-rate via data-driven engagement).
2. **Animated closed-loop revenue diagram** — the moat, made visceral: nodes pulse in sequence on
   scroll, showing the signal feeding back to Meta/Google. Replaces a wall of feature bullets.
3. **Before / After (or Human-Team vs AI-Workforce) split** — an interactive side-by-side toggle:
   the manual world (cold leads, 8-hr caps, attrition, blind ROI) vs the Famit world (instant calls,
   24/7, signal loop, revenue truth). Loss aversion made visual.
4. **Live proof artifacts inline** — an embedded real AI-call snippet / waveform + a real generated
   banner + the live per-call meter. "It's real, not a mock" — the strongest trust move for a young
   product.
5. **Anchor-hero-decoy pricing with a "what you replace" anchor band** — the human-team ₹-lakh cost
   shown directly above a polished 3-tier table (middle = hero), so the price always feels small.

(Rider innovations if room: a sticky CTA bar; a one-tap "personalize for `[business]`" hero token;
a print-to-PDF clean stylesheet so the same file doubles as a leave-behind.)

## 7. ANTI-PATTERNS (what makes a proposal feel cheap / lose the deal)

- Company-first ("about us" wall) before the buyer's problem. Lead with THEIR pain.
- Feature-dump pricing matrix with 30 rows; full price tables front-loaded.
- Fabricated testimonials/logos/metrics (instant credibility death for an SMB owner).
- Black-box "10× ROI guaranteed!" with no math. Show the transparent calculation instead.
- Over-animated / gradient-soup template look. Restraint = premium.
- Multiple competing CTAs. One next step.
- Skipping *agitate* (naming the problem then immediately pitching). No pain, no urgency.
- Over-claiming (pretending AI beats humans at everything) — name the honest handoff.
