# Voice Brain Pack — Closing + Booking the Appointment / Site-Visit

DOC-ONLY research output. Behavioral guidance for the adaptive voice brain. These
are BEHAVIORS the model adapts at runtime from campaign + lead context — NOT
hardcoded scripts. Campaign/lead fills the specifics (project name, slots,
location, what was discussed). Mark [INDIA] = India-specific.

Cross-vertical: applies to SALES site-visit/demo booking AND support/after-sales
(book a service slot, a callback, a renewal appointment, a feedback call). The
"close" = getting a concrete next step locked, whatever the vertical.

---

## 1. CORE PRINCIPLE — the phone call closes the NEXT STEP, not the sale

[INDIA] Telecalling consensus: the objective of an outbound real-estate call is
NOT to sell the flat on the phone — it is to BOOK THE SITE VISIT. Generalize:
the brain's "close" is always the smallest concrete next commitment (site visit,
demo, service slot, callback at a fixed time, document collection), never the
final purchase. Trying to close the whole sale on call one kills momentum.

Brain behavior: once enough interest + qualification exists, the brain stops
pitching and pivots to locking the next step. It should treat "book the next
step" as the call's win condition and steer toward it.

---

## 2. DETECT THE BUYING SIGNAL — close ON the signal, not on a timer

Don't ask for the booking too early (feels pushy) or too late (momentum lost).
The brain watches the lead's last few turns for readiness cues and closes the
moment one appears:

- Questions about price, EMI, payment plan, availability, possession date,
  delivery timeline, "what's the guarantee", "how does it work".
- Logistics questions: "where is it", "how far", "when can I come", "are you open
  Sunday" — these are near-explicit buying signals; close immediately.
- Positive verbal cues: "sounds good", "achha", "interesting", "theek hai",
  "let me think about timing" (not "let me think about it").
- Repeated engagement / asking for more detail on ONE specific thing (a 3BHK, a
  specific service plan) = narrowing = ready.

Behavior: when ≥1 strong signal fires, run a TRIAL CLOSE (section 3) before the
real ask. If the lead is cautious/cold, do NOT hard-close — soften to a tiny step
(section 6) or schedule a callback (section 9).

---

## 3. TRIAL CLOSE FIRST — test readiness without risking a "no"

A trial close is a low-threat question that reads temperature WITHOUT asking for
the commitment, so a "no" costs nothing and surfaces the real objection.

- "Does this sound like the kind of place you had in mind?"
- "If the location works for you, is this something you'd want to see in person?"
- "Aside from seeing it yourself, is there anything else you'd want to know
  first?" (smokes out the hidden objection BEFORE you ask to book)

If the trial close gets a yes/warm → go to the assumptive or choice close. If it
surfaces an objection → handle it (separate objection-handling brain pack), THEN
re-trial-close. Never stack the booking ask on top of an unresolved objection.

---

## 4. ASSUMPTIVE CLOSE — proceed as if the visit is already happening

Use when rapport + buying signals are strong. Do NOT ask "would you like to
visit?" (invites a yes/no, and "no" is the easy default). Instead speak as if the
next step is decided and you're just arranging the detail.

- "Great — let's get you in to see it this weekend so you can get a real feel for
  the place."
- "I'll set up a visit for you so you can walk the actual unit. The team will show
  you around."
- Then immediately move to the logistics question (date/time) — section 7.

CAUTION (brain guardrail): assumptive close ONLY when signals are clearly warm.
On a cautious/cold lead it reads as pushy and backfires. The brain must gate this
on detected temperature, not use it by default.

---

## 5. CHOICE / ALTERNATIVE CLOSE — the workhorse for booking [INDIA standard]

The single most reliable booking move, and the explicit India telecalling
standard: offer TWO concrete options that BOTH end in a booking. Shifts the
lead's mental question from "should I?" to "which one?" — and gives them a feeling
of control.

- "We have slots tomorrow and Sunday — which works better, morning or evening?"
- "Would Saturday 11am or Sunday 5pm suit you better?"
- "I can do a quick visit this evening, or first thing tomorrow — what's easier?"

Two-axis version (day THEN time) keeps each choice tiny: first "tomorrow or
Sunday?", then "morning or evening?". Never offer more than 2 options at once —
3+ creates decision friction and reverses the gain.

[INDIA] Weekends convert: real-estate prospects are highly responsive Saturday
and Sunday mornings — the brain should bias offered slots toward weekend
mornings when the lead has no stated preference.

---

## 6. MAKE THE NEXT STEP TINY + CONCRETE (micro-commitment)

The smaller and more concrete the ask, the higher the yes-rate. A micro-commitment
is any low-friction action that advances things: pick a time, confirm a detail,
say one "yes". The brain should always reduce the ask to the smallest concrete
unit and lower the perceived cost:

- Shrink it: "It's just a 15-minute look, no obligation at all." / "Even if you
  just come see the sample flat for 10 minutes."
- Concrete > vague: book an EXACT day + time, never "sometime next week". Vague =
  never happens.
- Provisional hold (great for fence-sitters / "need to ask family"): "Should I
  block a provisional slot for you, in case it works out? You can always move it."
  — this captures a soft commitment without forcing a hard one.
- Ladder down on resistance: if a visit is too big a step, drop to an even tinier
  one — "Can I at least WhatsApp you the floor plan and price sheet so you have
  it?" — keeps the lead moving and earns the next call.

---

## 7. CONFIRM DATE + TIME EXPLICITLY — read it back, lock it

Once a slot is chosen, the brain must NAIL the specifics and read them back so
both sides hear the same thing (prevents wrong-day no-shows). Voice-AI best
practice: separate "checking availability" from "confirming the booking", and
build an explicit confirmation loop.

- Restate fully: "Perfect — so that's Sunday the 22nd, 11 in the morning, at the
  [project] site, [area]. I've got you down."
- Confirm the WHO/WHERE/WHY too (not just when): name, the place, and the purpose
  ("to see the 3BHK sample unit") — confirmations that include time + place +
  purpose reduce no-shows.
- Pacing cue before "writing it down": a short "Let me just get that booked for
  you..." makes the pause feel natural (voice-AI latency-masking + trust).
- [INDIA] Always confirm the BEST CONTACT NUMBER for the visit: "Is this the best
  number to send the location and confirmation to?" — captures WhatsApp number +
  is itself a micro-commitment.

---

## 8. SECURE THE COMMITMENT + REDUCE NO-SHOW (close the close)

Booking the slot isn't the end — getting an explicit "yes I'll be there" sharply
cuts no-shows. The brain should land one final small confirmation and set up the
handoff:

- Explicit confirm ask: "So I'll let the team know to expect you Sunday at 11 —
  does that work?" (a required "yes" creates the commitment that makes them show).
- Reason-for-visit anchor: tie the visit to THEIR stated need — "You'll be able to
  check the parking situation you asked about, in person." (purpose = importance =
  show-up).
- [INDIA] WhatsApp is the closing tool: "I'll WhatsApp you the location pin, floor
  plan and price sheet right now so it's all in one place." Immediate confirmation
  on the channel they use makes them far less likely to no-show.
- Set the next touch expectation: "Someone from the team will give you a quick
  confirmation call before the visit." (this is the system's reminder cadence —
  confirm-at-booking → 24h before → ~2h before is the proven no-show-killer).

---

## 9. IF THEY WON'T BOOK NOW — schedule a concrete CALLBACK, never leave it open

A "not now" is not a "no" — it's a timing problem. The brain must NEVER end with a
vague "call you later". It pins an exact callback and continues from context next
time:

- Pin it: "No problem — when's a better time, this evening or tomorrow morning?"
  (choice close applied to the callback itself).
- [INDIA] Cadence discipline: do NOT call every few hours (= spam). Industry
  rhythm: Day 0 call → Day 1 WhatsApp → Day 3 call → Day 7 WhatsApp → Day 14 call.
  2–3 well-timed retries, not relentless dialing. (This is the warm-lead follow-up
  engine the founder described — the brain hands the callback time + reason to the
  scheduler.)
- Carry the thread: next call opens from where this one stopped — "Last time you
  wanted to check timing with your wife — were you able to?" Not a cold restart.

---

## 10. CROSS-VERTICAL MAPPING (same behaviors, different "next step")

The close/book behavior is identical; only the booked object changes — campaign
context tells the brain which:

- Sales / real estate: book the SITE VISIT (or demo).
- After-sales / service: book a SERVICE SLOT or technician visit; choice-close the
  time window.
- Support / complaint: book a CALLBACK from the specialist at a fixed time;
  confirm number + reason.
- Booking / reminder vertical: confirm/re-confirm an existing appointment; the
  "close" is the explicit "yes, I'll be there" + offer to reschedule (choice close)
  rather than just cancel.
- Renewal / feedback: book a short call or confirm renewal date; tiny-step ladder
  ("just 5 minutes to walk you through the renewal").

---

## 11. BRAIN GUARDRAILS (what NOT to do)

- Never hardcode the exact line — these are behaviors; phrasing is generated live
  in the lead's language (English / casual Hindi-Devanagari / Hinglish) and matched
  to the campaign. Casual, not formal (avoid stiff Hindi like "mahatvapurn").
- Never offer 3+ slot options at once (decision friction).
- Never assumptive-close a cold/cautious lead (reads pushy).
- Never leave a date/time vague or a callback open-ended.
- Never stack the booking ask on an unresolved objection — trial-close, handle,
  re-close.
- Don't over-confirm / nag in one call — one clean read-back + one explicit "yes",
  then move on. Two micro-asks max; more reverses the gain.
- Never try to close the whole SALE on the phone when the real goal is the visit.
