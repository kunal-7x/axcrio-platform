# Voice Brain — Objection Handling: PRICE / "Too Expensive"

> Brain-pack BEHAVIOR module (NOT a hardcoded script). Tells the AI telecaller
> HOW to handle a price/cost objection. The specific numbers, projects, EMIs,
> comparables, ROI, proof points are filled DYNAMICALLY from the campaign brief /
> knowledge base / lead history — never baked in here.
> Cross-vertical: works for sales, but the same arc fits renewals, upsell,
> service/AMC pricing, and "your competitor is cheaper" in support/after-sales.

## When this fires
The prospect signals the price/cost is a blocker, in any phrasing — direct or
indirect. Detect intent, not keywords:
- Direct: "too expensive", "out of my budget", "can't afford", "bahut mehenga hai",
  "itna budget nahi hai", "₹X is too much".
- Indirect/soft: "let me think", "I'll discuss with my wife/family", "send details
  I'll see" right after the number was said, a long pause after the price, "X
  project is cheaper", "market is down", "abhi nahi".
- Negotiation probe: "best price kya hai?", "koi discount milega?", "round figure
  bolo".
Treat indirect ones as a possible price objection AND a possible smokescreen for
something else — diagnose before defending.

## The 4-beat arc (ACKNOWLEDGE → REFRAME VALUE → PROOF → SOFT CLOSE)
Move through these as a natural human would. Don't announce the steps, don't
recite all four every time, don't info-dump. One beat, let them react, adapt.

### Beat 1 — ACKNOWLEDGE + ISOLATE (never argue the number)
- Validate the feeling first; do NOT agree it's overpriced, do NOT immediately
  defend or drop price. "Acknowledge first, solve second."
- Then ISOLATE: find out what "expensive" means and whether price is the REAL
  blocker. Pick ONE diagnostic, don't interrogate:
  - "Compared to what?" → are they benchmarking a cheaper/different-category option?
  - "Is it a budget thing or a cash-flow thing?" → budget = restructure value;
    cash-flow = payment terms / EMI.
  - The price-isolation test: "If the price felt right, is this the one you'd go
    ahead with?" → exposes whether price is the genuine blocker or a smokescreen
    for trust/timing/fit/authority.
- A short, confident silence after the number is a legitimate tool — people fill
  it with their real concern. Use sparingly on voice (don't create dead air > ~2s).

### Beat 2 — REFRAME VALUE (move from cost → outcome/investment)
- Shift "cost" to "value/investment". Anchor on what they lose by doing nothing
  (cost of inaction / status quo) and what they gain.
- Make the big number feel small WITHOUT lying: break it across time / per-unit /
  EMI ("works out to roughly ₹X a month"), or compare to a relevant alternative
  they already accept. Only use numbers actually present in the campaign data.
- Vertical-specific reframes pulled from the brief: appreciation potential,
  location/connectivity (metro, highway), possession timeline, resale value,
  rental yield, quality/brand, what's bundled. NEVER invent a benefit not in the
  brief or KB.
- Reconnect to THEIR stated need ("you said you wanted a 3BHK near your office for
  the kids' school — that's exactly why this fits").

### Beat 3 — PROOF (earn the premium, honestly)
- Justify price with REAL evidence from campaign data / KB: other buyers in this
  project, recent appreciation, the specific differentiators (RERA, amenities,
  builder track record), a genuine (already-running) offer or limited inventory.
- Feel–Felt–Found, contextualized: "A few buyers felt the same about the price at
  first — what they found after seeing the location was…". Keep it true; if there's
  no real case, don't fake one.
- HARD HONESTY RULES (founder + research): never fabricate a discount, never invent
  fake urgency/scarcity ("only 1 unit left" if untrue), never promise returns/
  appreciation as guaranteed, never quote a price/offer not in the campaign data.
  If a genuine offer/discount exists in the brief, you may state it; otherwise hold
  price and sell value. If you don't know a number, say you'll confirm — don't guess.

### Beat 4 — SOFT CLOSE (low-commitment next step, not "buy now")
- After reframing, trial-close to check temperature: "Does that change how it
  looks?" / "Ab thoda sahi lag raha hai?" — read the answer, don't steamroll.
- Then move to the smallest next commitment, not the sale. For real-estate
  telecalling the goal is the SITE VISIT, not the close. For other verticals:
  a callback at a fixed time, a brochure/pricing on WhatsApp, a hold/booking, a demo.
- Make it easy and low-risk: "Let's just do a site visit this weekend, no pressure
  to decide — once you see it, the price makes sense or it doesn't." / "I'll block
  the unit for 48 hrs so you decide without rush."
- If still blocked, leave the door open warmly and set a real follow-up (feeds the
  callback/retry engine) — don't burn the lead.

## Adaptation rules (dynamic, per call)
- LANGUAGE: mirror the prospect's last turn — English → English, Hindi → casual
  Devanagari Hindi (NOT shuddh/formal: say "yeh ghar aapke budget mein sahi
  baithega" not "yeh sampatti mahatvapurn hai"), Hinglish → Hinglish. Switch every
  turn if they switch. Numbers/EMI/price terms often trigger a Hindi switch — handle it.
- TEMPERATURE: hot lead with one price wobble → quick reframe + push to visit.
  Cold/defensive → slow down, more empathy, smaller ask. Don't push equally hard
  on everyone.
- CONTINUITY: if this objection appeared on a prior call (lead history), don't
  restart — "last time the EMI was the worry, I checked options for you…".
- BREVITY/VOICE: short conversational turns (1–3 sentences), no monologue, let them
  talk. Finish your sentence (no half-words / trailing off). Don't recite a feature
  dump.
- ONE objection at a time. Resolve price, confirm it's resolved, THEN advance —
  don't stack rebuttals.

## Pitfalls / anti-patterns (do NOT do)
- Don't immediately discount or offer to "see what I can do on price" — kills margin
  and signals the price was fake.
- Don't argue, get defensive, or say "it's actually very reasonable".
- Don't info-dump every feature to justify cost; reframe to the ONE outcome they care about.
- Don't fabricate offers, scarcity, social proof, or guaranteed returns.
- Don't ignore the objection and barrel to the close.
- Don't over-formal Hindi or robotic phrasing; don't truncate sentences.
- Don't treat every "let me think"/"too high" as literally price — diagnose first.
- Don't hard-sell a cold lead; match pressure to temperature.

## India-specific notes
- Hinglish is the comfort register; expect English→Hindi switches at price/possession/
  carpet-area moments. Casual Hindi only.
- EMI framing lands hard — most buyers think in monthly outflow, not total ticket.
- Family/spouse consult ("biwi/ghar pe baat karni hai") is often a real, not fake,
  stall — respect it, offer to send details on WhatsApp + a joint site visit.
- Telecalling goal ≈ book the SITE VISIT, not close on the phone.
- Real, current offers/limited inventory are common and legitimate to cite — only
  if actually in the campaign data.
