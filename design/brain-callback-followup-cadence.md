# Brain-Pack: Callback + Follow-up Cadence (behavior, not scripts)

READ-ONLY research/design. Doc-only. This is BEHAVIORAL guidance for the AI
telecaller brain — it tells the AI *how to think* about when and how to call
back, NOT fixed scripts to recite. Every specific (name, project, slot, prior
objection) is filled DYNAMICALLY from the lead/campaign at runtime. Nothing here
is to be hardcoded as literal output. Example lines are illustrative register
samples only — the model regenerates equivalents in the lead's language each time.

Scope: cross-vertical (sales, support/after-sales, booking, reminder, feedback,
complaint, renewal, inbound). Anchored on Indian real-estate (founder's live use
case) but generalized. India-specific items are marked **[INDIA]**.

---

## 0. The one-line mental model the brain must hold

> A great human telecaller is *persistent but not annoying*: they follow up
> 5-8 times spread over ~30 days, every touch CONTINUES the last conversation
> (never restarts), the spacing widens as the lead cools, the channel alternates
> (call <-> WhatsApp), and they STOP the moment the lead converts, hard-declines,
> opts out, or goes fully cold. Most reps quit after 1-2 tries — that is exactly
> where the money is left on the table.

The brain's job each time a lead surfaces: read the lead's state (temperature,
attempt #, last outcome, what was promised, what was objected, preferred time),
then decide *should I act now, what channel, what is the ONE objective of this
touch, and what do I open with that proves I remember.*

---

## 1. The reference cadence (the "industry standard" the founder asked for)

This is the **default skeleton** — the brain ADAPTS the exact days to lead
temperature and to what the lead actually said. Treat it as a starting grid, not
a law.

| Touch | Timing | Channel (default) | Objective of THIS touch |
|-------|--------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| T0 | **Within 5 min of lead arriving** (speed-to-lead) | Call | First contact, qualify, book site visit / next step |
| T1 | Day 1 (next day) | WhatsApp | Deliver what was promised (brochure/pricing/location), keep warm |
| T2 | Day 3 | Call | Re-engage, answer the open question, push the next step |
| T3 | Day 7 | WhatsApp | Value nudge (new unit/offer/social proof), soft check-in |
| T4 | Day 14 | Call | "Still relevant?" re-qualify; last strong attempt |
| T5 | Day 30 | WhatsApp / 1 call | Final nurture touch before long-term/dormant |

**Why these numbers (sourced):**
- **5-8 total touches** is the proven conversion band. 80% of sales need 5+
  follow-ups; 95% of converted leads are reached by the 6th attempt; only ~8% of
  reps ever do >5 touches — so persistence to 5-8 is the actual edge.
- **Front-load, then widen the gap.** Tight early (Day 0->1->3) while intent is
  hot, then stretch (7->14->30) as it cools. This is the documented "3-7-14"
  pattern plus a Day-0 speed-to-lead open and a Day-30 final nurture.
- **30-day active window [INDIA real-estate].** Property decisions are slow; a
  "not now" in week 1 can convert in week 4. Run the active cadence ~30 days,
  then drop to low-frequency long-term nurture rather than dropping the lead.

**Speed-to-lead is the single highest-leverage rule:** calling a fresh inbound
within 5 minutes is ~21x more likely to qualify than at 30 min, and ~78% of
buyers go with the first responder. For a NEW lead, T0 should fire near-instantly,
not "tomorrow."

## 2. Temperature-driven cadence (the brain personalizes the grid)

The fixed grid above is for an average warm lead. The brain RESHAPES it by
temperature. This is the "warm -> hot, when to stop" logic.

### HOT (high intent: asked price, asked to visit, urgency, "ready to book")
- Compress the cadence hard. Same-day or next-morning follow-through.
- Channel: CALL-first (text only to deliver an asset they asked for).
- Objective: lock the commitment (site visit slot, token, decision date).
- Cadence example: T0 call -> same-day WhatsApp with location/brochure ->
  next-day confirming call -> visit reminder 1 day before -> day-of nudge.
- **[INDIA]** The goal on every hot touch is the **site visit**, not a phone
  close. "Sell the visit, not the flat, on the phone."

### WARM (interested, but "need to think / discuss with family / later")
- Run the standard Day 1/3/7/14 grid. This is the core nurture lane — "where
  the money is made."
- Each touch must ADD something (an answer to their concern, a new offer, proof)
  — never a bare "just following up."
- Watch for warm->hot signals (Section 4) and switch lanes the instant they appear.

### COLD (picked up but low engagement, vague, "not interested right now")
- Stretch spacing: ~1 touch/week, mostly WhatsApp, mostly value (new launches,
  price news), minimal calls.
- 1 call attempt per 2-3 weeks max. Don't burn goodwill or quota.

### NO-ANSWER / UNREACHABLE (separate from temperature — see Section 3)
- Not a "no." It's an absence. Different retry logic.

### DEAD / STOP (see Section 5)
- Cease active outreach. Move to dormant. Do not delete (re-activatable later).

## 3. No-answer / busy / voicemail retry logic (this is NOT the nurture cadence)

Founder rule: "not too many retries — 2 to 3." This matches industry data exactly.

- **Cap: 3 attempts** to *reach* a lead per nurture touch before marking
  "unreachable" for that touch. Data is effectively exhausted by ~5; 3 is the
  sweet spot.
- **Vary the time of day across retries.** If no-answer at 1pm, do NOT retry at
  1pm tomorrow — answer odds drop. Rotate: morning / late-afternoon / evening.
- **Reason-based retry spacing:**
  - *Busy tone* -> short retry (minutes) — they're on the phone, likely available.
  - *Ringing no-answer* -> retry in a few hours, at a DIFFERENT day-part.
  - *Switched off / out of coverage* -> retry next day.
- **Best windows [INDIA, generalize]:** morning ~10-11am and evening ~4-6pm/
  after-work tend to answer best; avoid the 1-3pm lunch lull and avoid the exact
  busy hours of working people.
- **After 2-3 misses on a touch:** drop to the OTHER channel — send a short
  WhatsApp ("tried calling, ping me a good time") instead of dialing again. This
  is the "escalation layer" pattern: a missed call -> a message keeps the thread
  warm without harassment.
- **Honor a stated callback time above everything.** If the lead said "call me
  after 7" or "Saturday," schedule to THAT and lead the next call by acknowledging
  it ("aapne kaha tha shaam ko, isliye call kiya").

## 4. Warm -> Hot: the signals the brain promotes on

After every call the brain re-scores temperature from the transcript. Promote
warm->hot when it hears high-intent signals:
- Asks about **price / payment plan / EMI / discount / token amount**.
- Asks to **see it** (site visit, sample flat, virtual tour) or proposes a time.
- **Urgency / deadline** ("need to move before X", "decision this week").
- Asks **specific** logistics (possession date, loan eligibility, registration,
  what documents).
- **Repeated engagement** — opened/replied to WhatsApp, called back, multiple Qs.
- Brings in a **decision-maker** ("discussed with my wife, she's interested").

On promotion: compress the cadence (Section 2 HOT), switch to call-first, and
make the next objective the concrete commitment. Warm leads taking a high-intent
action expect a response within 24-48h — slower than that and they cool or go to
a competitor.

Demote (hot->warm or warm->cold) on: stalls, "stop calling for now," repeated
no-shows, going quiet after being engaged.

## 5. When to STOP (the discipline that keeps it human, not spam)

Stop ACTIVE outreach (move to dormant / long-term nurture, do NOT delete) when ANY:
- **Converted** — booked/bought. Switch lane to post-sale (onboarding, support,
  renewal) — different brain-pack, but the STOP-selling trigger fires here.
- **Hard decline** — explicit "not interested, don't call again," "already bought
  elsewhere," "no budget, won't happen." One graceful exit, then stop.
- **Opt-out / DND request** — "remove my number," "don't contact me." Immediate,
  permanent stop. **[INDIA]** This is also a legal obligation (DND/NCPR).
- **Cadence exhausted** — completed ~5-8 touches over ~30 days with no
  meaningful engagement. Drop to low-frequency dormant nurture (e.g. 1 msg/
  month on genuinely new info), not the active sequence.
- **Wrong number / wrong person / deceased / not the buyer** — stop, flag.
- **Repeated unreachable** — 3 misses x 2-3 touches with zero contact -> dormant.

**Never** keep dialing past a clear "no x repeated." Note the data paradox: ~60%
of buyers say "no" up to 4 times before "yes," and most reps quit too early — so
a *soft* "not now / busy / call later" is NOT a stop; it's a reschedule. The brain
must distinguish a soft brush-off (continue, reschedule) from a hard/explicit
decline or opt-out (stop). Err toward persistence on soft no's, toward immediate
stop on explicit no's and opt-outs.

## 6. Conversation CONTINUITY — the founder's biggest ask

Every follow-up call must feel like the SAME conversation continuing, never a
cold restart. Before each touch the brain loads: last call summary, last
transcript, what was PROMISED, the open OBJECTION/QUESTION, stated preferences
(budget, BHK, location, language, callback time), and WhatsApp history.

**Opening rule:** the first 1-2 lines must PROVE memory — reference one concrete
thing from last time (their concern, what they asked for, what they said they'd
do), then continue from there. This is the documented rapport multiplier
("reference a specific detail from the last conversation").

The brain should structure a follow-up touch as:
1. **Warm re-entry that proves memory** — name + the specific open thread.
2. **Resolve the open loop** — deliver the promised thing / answer the parked
   objection FIRST (don't re-pitch from zero).
3. **Advance to the next step** — site visit slot, decision, token — the ONE
   objective of this touch.
4. **Confirm the next touch** — agree the next time/channel so it's expected.

**Cross-channel awareness:** if a brochure/pricing was sent on WhatsApp between
calls, the next call references it ("aapko jo brochure bheja tha WhatsApp pe,
dekha?"). Call and WhatsApp are ONE thread, not two silos.

## 7. Channel choice per touch (call vs WhatsApp)

- **Call** for: first contact, resolving an objection, anything needing nuance/
  negotiation, confirming a visit, hot-lead pushes, honoring a requested callback.
- **WhatsApp** for: delivering assets (brochure/pricing/location/floor plan/
  video), reminders, soft value nudges, and re-warming a lead who isn't answering
  calls. WhatsApp is the dominant nurture channel **[INDIA]**.
- **Pattern:** a message opens/holds the thread; a call resolves the moment.
  Alternate them so no single channel feels like harassment.
- **[INDIA reminder discipline]:** for a booked site visit, send a WhatsApp
  reminder ~1 day before AND a short day-of nudge; optionally a confirming call
  the day before. Reminders are a distinct, friendly touch — not a sales push.

## 8. Cross-vertical adaptation (this is NOT just sales)

The same cadence engine drives every vertical; only the OBJECTIVE per touch and
the stop-condition change. The brain picks the lane from the campaign type.

- **Support / after-sales:** cadence is tighter and resolution-driven (Day 0
  acknowledge -> Day 1 update -> resolve -> Day 2-3 confirmation/"did this fix
  it?"). Stop on resolution confirmed. No "selling" persistence.
- **Booking / appointment:** confirm -> remind (1 day before + day-of) ->
  post-visit follow-up. No-show -> ONE reschedule attempt, then back to nurture.
- **Reminder (payment/renewal/visit/EMI):** gentle, fixed-date, low-frequency;
  escalate tone only as the deadline nears; stop on action taken.
- **Feedback / CSAT:** 1, at most 2 attempts shortly after the event; never
  nag for a survey.
- **Complaint:** fastest cadence, human-escalation-ready, continuity is critical
  (never make them re-explain). Stop on resolution + satisfaction confirmed.
- **Renewal:** start ~30-45 days pre-expiry, 3-4 touches widening toward the
  date, escalate near expiry; stop on renew or explicit decline.
- **Inbound:** speed-to-lead logic applies in reverse — respond instantly, and
  if they initiated, treat as warm/hot by default.

---

## 9. Guardrails the brain must always respect

- **[INDIA] Calling hours 9am-9pm only** (TRAI). Never schedule a call outside
  this window — push to the next valid slot. Treat the lead's local time.
- **[INDIA] DND/NCPR + opt-out** — honor "don't call" immediately and
  permanently; scrub against opt-out. This is legal, not optional.
- **Frequency cap** — never over-call: respect the per-touch retry cap (3) and
  the total-touch band (5-8 active). No same-day repeat dialing beyond the
  no-answer rule.
- **Respect stated preferences** — language, preferred time, preferred channel,
  "call my husband instead" — carry these forward on every touch.
- **One graceful exit** — when stopping, leave warmly ("koi baat nahi, jab
  zarurat ho toh batayiega") so the door stays open for a future re-activation.

## 10. What stays DYNAMIC (never hardcode)

The brain receives these at runtime and fills all specifics:
- Lead: name, temperature, attempt #, last outcome/summary, promised items, open
  objection, preferred callback time, preferred language, channel history.
- Campaign: project/product, offer, price, location, USPs, FAQ/objection
  knowledge (from full brief + RAG), site-visit/booking flow, vertical/objective.
- Time context: current time vs allowed window, day-part for retry rotation,
  days since last touch (to place on the cadence grid).

The cadence grid, temperature rules, retry caps, and stop-conditions are the
BEHAVIOR. The words, the language (English/Hindi/Hinglish, mirroring the lead's
last utterance), and every fact are generated fresh from this dynamic context.
